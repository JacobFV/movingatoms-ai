Scan to BIM for MEP

MEP is where existing buildings hide the most surprises. Scanning ceiling voids, risers and plant rooms before designing new services avoids clashes that otherwise appear on site. Here is how to do it well.

Why MEP needs Scan to BIM most

Services change constantly through a building's life and are rarely recorded. Drawings may show the original design, not the duct rerouted ten years later. When new services are designed against old drawings, clashes show up on site, where they are most expensive to fix. A scan of the actual ceiling void, modeled as real ducts, pipes and trays, lets designers route new work around what is really there.

Capturing MEP

  • Open the ceilings. Remove tiles in a grid pattern, or scan before ceilings go back in during strip-out.
  • Scan from below and between. Dense services shadow each other; plan extra positions or routes. Mobile and handheld scanners help in tight spaces (see choosing a scanner).
  • Plant rooms and risers need their own plan, access and often a different scanner.
  • Take photos of labels, tags and nameplates; they help identify systems and equipment.

LOD for MEP

LODWhat MEP looks likeGood for
LOD 200Approximate runs and boxes for equipmentSpace planning
LOD 300Measured size, position and routing of main runs and equipmentDesign and most coordination
LOD 350Adds connections, supports, clearances and interfaces to other systemsClash detection, detailed coordination
LOD 400Fabrication-level detailPrefabrication of replacements

For most retrofits, LOD 300 for distribution and LOD 350 for plant rooms and congested zones is the right balance. Set size thresholds, for example "pipes 50 mm and over". See LOD levels explained.

What to model

  • Ductwork with sizes, fittings and terminals.
  • Pipework by system with diameters, valves and insulation where visible.
  • Cable trays, ladders and containment.
  • Equipment: AHUs, pumps, chillers, boilers, panels, each as an individual element.
  • Supports and hangers where they affect clearances (LOD 350).
  • Systems assigned so the model can be filtered and checked by system.

Hidden services

Services buried in walls, slabs and shafts cannot be scanned. Agree how they will be handled: excluded, inferred from visible ends and marked as such, or surveyed by other methods. Never let inferred geometry look measured. See how to check a Scan to BIM model.

Using the model for coordination

Link the existing-services model into the design model and run clash detection in Navisworks or Revit. Clashes against existing services found in the model cost minutes; the same clash found on site costs days.

Frequently asked questions

What LOD should MEP Scan to BIM use?

LOD 300 for distribution and LOD 350 for plant rooms and congested zones suits most retrofit coordination.

Can laser scanning see services above ceilings?

Only if the ceiling is open or tiles are removed. Services inside walls and slabs cannot be scanned and must be inferred or surveyed by other means.
