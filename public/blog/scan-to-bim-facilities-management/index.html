Scan to BIM for facilities management

Most buildings were handed over without a usable model. Scan to BIM gives owners and facilities teams one after the fact: every asset located, identified and ready to connect to maintenance and space systems.

Why owners scan existing buildings

Facilities teams manage thousands of assets: air handlers, pumps, fire equipment, lighting, doors, furniture. Records are often spreadsheets that do not say where anything is. A model built from a scan places every asset in 3D with its type and properties, which makes maintenance planning, space management and capital planning much easier.

What a facilities model needs

  • Assets as individual objects, not merged geometry, so each can carry an ID and data.
  • Spaces and rooms with names, numbers and areas.
  • Consistent classification of asset types.
  • Properties that link to your CAFM or CMMS system: asset tag, manufacturer, model, service dates.
  • Modest geometry. LOD 200 to 300 is usually enough; information matters more than detail (see LOD levels explained).

Standards that help

  • ISO 19650 defines the asset information model (AIM) and the asset information requirements that describe it. The CDBB guidance on asset information requirements is a practical starting point.
  • COBie is a widely used format for handing asset data to facilities systems.
  • IFC is the open format to keep the model in for the long term (see IFC or Revit?).

A practical process

  1. Define what you need: which asset types, which properties, which system they feed.
  2. Scan the building; mobile scanners are efficient for large portfolios (see choosing a scanner).
  3. Model spaces and assets, with each asset separated.
  4. Populate identifiers and link to existing asset registers.
  5. Export IFC or COBie and import into your facilities system.
  6. Rescan changed areas after major works to keep the model current.

From model to digital twin

A digital twin connects the model to live data: sensors, work orders, occupancy. The scan-based model is the spatial backbone. Twins built on OpenUSD can also be used for simulation, for example testing how robots for cleaning, delivery or inspection would move through the building (see from Scan to BIM to SimReady).

Frequently asked questions

How is Scan to BIM used in facilities management?

A scan-based model locates every asset and space in 3D with identifiers and properties, which feed maintenance, space management and capital planning systems.

What LOD is needed for facilities management?

Usually LOD 200 to 300. Separated assets and good data matter more than geometric detail.

What is COBie?

COBie (Construction-Operations Building information exchange) is a widely used format for handing building asset data to facilities management systems.
