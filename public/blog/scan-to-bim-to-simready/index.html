From Scan to BIM to SimReady

A good Scan to BIM model already contains most of what a robot simulation needs: every object measured and separated. A few more steps turn it into a SimReady world where robots are trained and tested before they ever reach the site.

Why robotics teams care about Scan to BIM

Robots fail in the gap between the lab and the real site: the aisle is narrower, the shelf higher, the floor rougher. Testing in a simulated copy of the real site catches those problems early. Building that copy by hand is slow; building it from a scan, using the same separated objects a BIM team would use, is fast.

What "SimReady" means

NVIDIA uses SimReady for 3D assets that are ready for physically accurate simulation, built on OpenUSD. Compared with a BIM model, a SimReady world adds:

PropertyBIM modelSimReady world
Separated objectsYes (at good LOD)Required
Visual geometryYesYes, with real materials
Collision geometryNoSimplified shapes for physics
Mass and frictionNoPer object
Moving partsRarelyDoors, drawers, hinges, joints
FormatRevit, IFCOpenUSD
Semantic labelsCategoriesLabels for perception training

The extra steps

  1. Separate everything. A robot needs every box, chair and tool as its own body. This is where object-level Scan to BIM pays off.
  2. Add collision shapes that are simple enough for fast, stable physics.
  3. Assign physics materials: mass, friction, restitution.
  4. Articulate moving parts: door hinges, drawer slides, lids.
  5. Export to OpenUSD with semantic labels, ready for simulators such as NVIDIA Isaac Sim.
  6. Randomize. Vary layouts, lighting and object placement so robots generalize beyond one fixed copy.

OpenUSD is becoming a standard

The Alliance for OpenUSD released OpenUSD Core Specification 1.0 in December 2025. Just as IFC is the open record for buildings (see IFC or Revit?), USD is becoming the open format for simulation.

What people do with it

  • Plan where robots, conveyors and charging stations go in a real factory or warehouse (see Scan to BIM for industrial plants).
  • Train and evaluate robot policies on the real site before deployment.
  • Generate synthetic training data with realistic variation.
  • Run digital twins for facilities operations (see Scan to BIM for facilities management).

Frequently asked questions

What is a SimReady asset?

A 3D asset prepared for physically accurate simulation, typically in OpenUSD, with real materials, collision geometry, physics properties and semantic labels.

Can a Revit model be used for robot simulation?

It is a good starting point. It needs collision shapes, physics materials, articulation of moving parts and export to OpenUSD to become simulation-ready.
