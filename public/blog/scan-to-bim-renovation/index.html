Scan to BIM for renovation and retrofit

Renovation design is only as good as its existing-conditions survey. Scan to BIM gives architects and engineers a measured starting point. Here is how to use it from feasibility to handover.

Why scan before you renovate

Old buildings are rarely as drawn. Walls are out of plumb, floors are not level, and decades of alterations have moved things around. Designing against drawings that are wrong leads to redesign, variations and delays. A measured model removes the guesswork, and becomes the base for the design, the planning drawings and the record of the finished work.

When to scan

  • Feasibility: a quick, lower-LOD model to test options. Even a phone scan can help for small spaces (see Scan to BIM with an iPhone).
  • Design: a full existing-conditions model at LOD 300.
  • After strip-out: a second scan once finishes are removed reveals structure and services hidden before.
  • Completion: an as-built scan to verify and record the finished work.

What to model

ElementSuggested LODNotes
Walls, floors, roofs300Model real thickness and out-of-plumb where it matters
Doors, windows, openings300Sizes, sill and head heights
Structure300Visible members; flag inferred ones
Services300 to 350See Scan to BIM for MEP
Heritage featuresMesh or 400Mouldings and ornament as measured meshes, not families

Existing and new in one model

In Revit, put the scanned elements on an "Existing" phase and design on a "New Construction" phase. Demolition is shown by setting elements to be demolished in the new phase. This keeps the survey intact and produces existing, demolition and proposed drawings from one model. Ask for this in the specification (see how to write a Scan to BIM specification).

Irregular geometry

Should a wall that leans 20 mm be modeled leaning? Usually no for design work, yes where fit matters. Decide per element and set the LOA accordingly. The deviation analysis (see QA against the point cloud) will show where the simplification exceeds tolerance.

Historic buildings

Scans are an excellent record of historic fabric. Ornamental elements are best kept as measured meshes linked to simple BIM elements, so the model stays light while the record stays accurate.

Energy retrofits

For envelope upgrades, the scan gives exact facade areas, window sizes and roof geometry for energy models and for ordering insulation and glazing. Pairing the model with thermal imaging helps locate weak spots.

Frequently asked questions

Why use Scan to BIM for renovation?

Existing buildings are rarely as drawn. A measured model avoids designing against wrong drawings, reducing redesign and site variations.

How do I show existing and new in Revit?

Model the scanned elements on an Existing phase and the design on a New Construction phase, using phase filters for existing, demolition and proposed views.
