How AI is automating Scan to BIM

Why modeling was always the bottleneck

Scanning a building takes hours to days. Turning the scan into a model has traditionally taken weeks, because a person traces every wall, pipe and piece of equipment against the cloud in Revit (see how to convert a point cloud to Revit). Automation aims at that step.

The automated pipeline

  1. Pre-processing. The registered cloud is cleaned, subsampled and split into manageable regions, and floors and rooms are found.
  2. Semantic segmentation. A neural network labels every point: wall, floor, ceiling, door, window, column, duct, pipe, cable tray, equipment, furniture. Architectures such as Point Transformer V3, and open codebases such as Pointcept, are the research foundation.
  3. Instance separation. Points with the same label are split into individual objects: this pump, that pump, each run of duct.
  4. Element fitting. Each object becomes a BIM element. Planar elements are fitted as walls and slabs with thickness and height; pipes and ducts as runs with a diameter or section; equipment is matched to a family or modeled as measured geometry.
  5. Relationships. Elements are hosted on levels, openings cut into walls, and runs connected, so the model behaves like a model rather than a pile of shapes.
  6. Review and QA. Results are checked against the cloud (see how to check a Scan to BIM model) and corrected where needed.

Where automation is strong

  • Planar architecture. Walls, floors, ceilings and slabs are found reliably.
  • Repetitive MEP. Straight pipe and duct runs and cable trays.
  • Volume. Thousands of objects that a person would never have time to separate, like every shelf, cabinet and fixture.
  • Consistency. The same rules everywhere, with no fatigue at the end of a long job.

Where people are still needed

  • What the scanner did not see. No algorithm measures inside a wall. Hidden geometry is inferred and must be flagged.
  • Ambiguity. Is it a duct or a boxed-in beam? Context, photos and judgement settle it.
  • Design intent. Choosing the right family, type and parameters for your template.
  • Sign-off. Someone has to accept the model against the specification.

How to evaluate an AI Scan to BIM provider

  1. Send your own scan. Demos on vendor data prove little. Ask for a pilot on a representative part of your site.
  2. Open the native model. Check that walls are walls (not generic models), openings cut, levels set and elements editable.
  3. Ask for a deviation report against the agreed LOA (see how to write a Scan to BIM specification).
  4. Count the objects. Is equipment separated into individual elements, or merged into a mesh?
  5. Check formats. Revit and IFC as a minimum; USD if you need a digital twin.
  6. Check data handling. Where is the scan processed and stored, and can it stay on site?

Frequently asked questions

Can AI convert a point cloud to a Revit model?

Yes. Automated pipelines segment the point cloud into elements and fit BIM objects to them, with people reviewing the result. Hidden geometry still has to be inferred and flagged.

Is automated Scan to BIM accurate?

Accuracy is limited by the scan and checked with a deviation analysis against the point cloud. Ask any provider for a deviation report against the agreed LOA.

How fast is automated Scan to BIM?

Moving Atoms delivers within 48 hours. Assisted tools reduce manual modeling time, but your team still does the modeling.
