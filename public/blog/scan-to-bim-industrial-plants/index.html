Scan to BIM for industrial plants and factories

Industrial sites are the hardest buildings to model and the most expensive to get wrong. A brownfield change is planned against a live plant, often with a short shutdown window. Here is how Scan to BIM works for factories, plants and warehouses.

Why industrial sites are different

  • Density. A plant room or process area can hold more objects per square meter than an entire office floor. Area-based pricing breaks down (see the Scan to BIM cost guide).
  • Live operations. Scanning has to fit around production and safety rules.
  • Short shutdowns. Tie-ins and installations must fit first time, so the model has to be right.
  • Many changes. Lines are reconfigured often; the record falls behind quickly.

Capture

Mobile SLAM scanners cover large factory and warehouse floors fast; a peer-reviewed study found indoor accuracy around 5 mm for leading devices (see choosing a scanner). Use terrestrial scanners where tie-ins need tight tolerances, and plan extra positions around dense pipe racks. Coordinate with site safety for access, permits and moving equipment.

Building shell and structureGrids, columns, crane rails and clearances

EquipmentEach machine and vessel as its own element with nozzle locations where needed
Conveyors and linesRoutes, heights and interfaces
Warehouse rackingBays, levels and aisle widths
FloorsFlatness and level for new equipment and robots

What the model is used for

  • Line changes and new equipment layouts.
  • Tie-in design and prefabrication of spools.
  • Clash checks for new services (see Scan to BIM for MEP).
  • Safety and egress studies.
  • Robot and automation deployment, where a physics-ready copy of the site is used to plan and test robots (see from Scan to BIM to SimReady).

Data security

Factory scans can be commercially sensitive. Ask where data is processed and stored. Moving Atoms offers on-site processing so scans do not leave your network.

Frequently asked questions

Can you laser scan a factory while it is running?

Usually yes. Scanning is planned around production and safety rules, and mobile scanners reduce time on the floor.

Is Scan to BIM priced per square foot for plants?

Often not. Industrial areas are dense, so pricing based on area underestimates the work. Expect pricing by scope and density.
